What each knife cuts

  • Lemongrass, sliced fine for rendang and tom yam. The long edge takes a whole bundle of stalks in one stroke.

  • Shallots, sliced thin for sambal and bawang goreng. The narrow tip works close to your fingers without crushing the layers.

  • Cabbage, shredded fine for kerabu and acar. The flat edge meets the board along its whole length, so every strand comes free.

  • Garlic, minced fine for every tumis. The tall blade scoops the pile off the board and into the wok.

  • Lime leaves, cut into threads for rendang and kerabu. Roll the leaves tight and let the tip do the work.

  • Fish fillet, sliced thin for bubur ikan. One long pull and no sawing, so the flesh stays whole.

  • Beef, sliced thin across the grain for daging masak kicap. Long, even strokes from heel to tip.

The chef

Halus is run by a chef who has cooked in Malaysian kitchens for fourteen years, from hotel banquets to a hawker's wok. Every knife here is one the chef wanted on the line: thin, hard and quick to bring back on the stone.

Looking after it

Wash it by hand and dry it at once. Keep it away from bones and frozen food. Once a month, a few strokes on a 1000 grit stone bring the edge back.
